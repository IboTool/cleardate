ClearDate help

What ClearDate is

ClearDate is a free reference tool for iboga and ibogaine providers. It supports patient intake and treatment planning.

You enter a patient's recent medications and substances. ClearDate plots how much of each drug and its metabolites is likely to remain in the body over time, relative to the planned ibogaine date. Underneath the main chart it shows when CYP2D6 is likely to be inhibited, which compounds carry QT liability, and which effects may linger after a drug has cleared.

Important — read before use

  • ClearDate is a reference and planning aid only. It is not medical advice, not a medical device, and must not be relied on for clinical decisions or dosing.
  • It does not replace clinical judgment, patient history, ECG monitoring, laboratory testing, drug screening or established clinical protocols.
  • Pharmacokinetic values are population estimates. Individual patients can differ substantially: genetics, liver and kidney function, age, body composition, drug interactions, adherence and product variation all matter. Self-reported doses may be inaccurate.
  • The model plots the estimated amount remaining in the body, not blood concentration. It does not predict effects, toxicity or QT intervals in milliseconds.
  • A “clear” date means the modelled amount has fallen below a chosen threshold. It does not mean that a drug's effects, withdrawal or receptor changes have resolved.
  • Where data is missing, the tool shows this rather than guessing. “Incomplete” and “with assumptions” statuses must be read, not ignored.
  • Ibogaine carries serious cardiac risk. Nothing in this tool indicates that treatment is safe for a given patient.
  • ClearDate is provided as-is, without warranty of any kind, under the MIT licence. You are responsible for complying with the laws and regulations that apply where you practise.

Privacy

  • All patient data stays in your browser. There are no accounts, no server, no analytics, no tracking and no cookies. Nothing you enter is uploaded. The only file the app downloads is its own drug library.
  • Everything you enter is autosaved in this browser only, on this computer. The indicator next to the logo is a reminder.
  • Saved patient files are files on your own computer. Treat them like any other patient record.
  • On a shared computer, use Settings → Clear all data at the end of each session. The browser asks you to confirm, and the deletion cannot be undone.
  • The code is open source, so these claims can be checked.

Quick start

  1. Enter the patient's details. Open the Patient tab and fill in what you have. Only the CYP2D6 phenotype changes the curves; the baseline QTc is shown on the QT track. See Patient details.
  2. Add each dose. In the Doses tab, start typing a drug's name, brand or street name and press Enter on the right product. Enter the amount and the date and time, or switch to Regimen for repeated doses, then press Add dose.
  3. Set the ibogaine treatment. Press ✦ Ibogaine treatment, enter the flood dose and its date and time, add any boosters, and save. The flood time is the date everything else is measured against.
  4. Read the chart and the statuses. Each product in the legend shows its clear date or status. Hover over the chart for details at any moment. Read every “Incomplete” and “with assumptions” status. See Clear statuses.
  5. Check the CYP2D6 and QT tracks under the chart, and the warnings below them. A red warning means CYP2D6 is likely inhibited at the treatment time.
  6. Export a record. PDF creates a report with the chart, the doses, the clear dates, every assumption and the warnings. PNG saves an image of the chart.
  7. Save or clear. Use Save file to keep a copy of the patient on your computer. On a shared computer, use Settings → Clear all data when you finish.

Using ClearDate

Patient details

The Patient tab holds:

  • Name: used on exports and as the file name of saved patient files.
  • Date of birth: the age is calculated and shown underneath.
  • Sex: shown on exports only.
  • Body weight in kg or lb: used only to show doses in mg/kg. It does not change the curves.
  • CYP2D6 phenotype: poor, intermediate, normal, ultrarapid or unknown. It scales the half-life of drugs that CYP2D6 clears and how much of some metabolites is formed. Unknown widens the range bands: the fast edge is simulated as an ultrarapid metaboliser and the slow edge as a poor metaboliser.
  • Baseline QTc in ms and the correction formula (Fridericia or Bazett): shown as a label on the QT track and on exports. It is not used in any calculation.
  • Notes: free text, kept with the patient and in saved files.

Finding drugs

Click the search box in the Doses tab and start typing. The search covers names, brand names, abbreviations, street names and drug classes, and tolerates small spelling mistakes. Results show which name matched.

  • Use the arrow keys and Enter to pick a result, or click it. Escape closes the list.
  • With the box empty, the list shows recently used products, then every product by category. Click a category heading to collapse it.
  • The coloured chips at the top filter by category. You can select more than one.
  • Each drug is listed per route and formulation (for example oral immediate release, oral extended release, patch or depot injection), because these clear differently.
  • If nothing matches, you're offered to add the name as a custom drug.

Badges next to a product:

BadgeMeaning
unverifiedLibrary data that has not been fully reviewed yet.
draftThe entry still contains values marked as drafts.
no data yetThe drug is in the library but has no half-life yet. It is listed so you know it's coming, but can't be selected.
incompleteThe drug can be plotted, but some compound it forms has no kinetics yet. Its clear date will be a lower bound.
user-enteredA custom drug you entered yourself. It is not part of the shared library.
verified — incomplete dataFully reviewed, but the kinetics of at least one compound are confirmed as unpublished. The clear date is a lower bound.

Entering doses

After you pick a product, a form opens under the search box. The amount is always in the product's dose unit, shown in the label. If the library has a typical dose, it is shown as a hint in the empty box. With the patient's weight set, the mg/kg equivalent appears in gold under the amount.

  • Single dose: one amount at one date and time.
  • Regimen: the same amount repeated every so many hours or days, from a start to an end date and time. The number of doses is shown under the dates. Enter a taper as several regimens, one per step.

Press Enter in the amount box to move to the date, and Enter in the date to add the dose. A note at the top of the form warns if some compound in the drug has no kinetics yet.

Combination products

For a product with more than one ingredient, such as buprenorphine/naloxone, you enter the amount of the first ingredient. The others are calculated from the product's fixed ratio and shown under the amount: 8 mg of buprenorphine/naloxone (4:1) is 8 mg buprenorphine + 2 mg naloxone. Each ingredient is plotted separately.

Patches, infusions and depot injections

  • Patches and infusions are entered as a rate (for example µg/h). A Single patch has an Applied at and a Removed at time. Patch changes repeats a patch every so many hours, each worn for a set number of hours. Release from the patch continues after removal, as described in How the model works.
  • Depot injections are entered as the amount per injection, as a single injection or repeated injections. The drug is released slowly from the injection site.
  • If the library doesn't have a release half-life for a modified-release product yet, the form says so and the product is simulated as immediate release.

Editing and deleting

Entered doses are listed under Entered doses, grouped by category, with each product's status underneath. Use ✎ to edit a dose and 🗑 to delete it. Hover over a status for the full list of assumptions.

If you leave a dose form half-filled and open this help page, the form is still there when you come back.

The ibogaine treatment

Open it with ✦ Ibogaine treatment at the top of the app or under the search box.

  • Flood dose: amount in mg and date and time. This is the planned treatment date: the bold gold line on the chart and the time every warning is checked against. A new treatment starts 14 days from now.
  • Boosters: + Add booster adds one 6 hours after the previous dose. They appear as thin dashed gold lines.
  • mg/kg is shown in gold under each amount when the patient's weight is set.
  • Projected ibogaine and noribogaine are plotted forward from the doses once the library has their kinetics. Until then only the date markers are shown, and a note says so. If CYP2D6 is likely inhibited at the treatment time, the projection uses the poor-metaboliser values and is labelled “poor-metaboliser adjusted”.
  • Remove treatment deletes it.

Reading the chart

Time runs left to right. The vertical axis is the estimated amount remaining in the body, in mg.

  • Lines: a solid line for each drug taken; dashed lines for its metabolites, lighter and more finely dashed for each further step in the chain.
  • Bands: the shaded area around a line runs from the fastest to the slowest clearance in the published range. The line is the typical value.
  • Today is a dotted line; the ibogaine treatment is a bold gold line, with boosters as thin dashed gold lines.
  • Clear ticks along the bottom mark each product's clear date: “✓ name”, “✓ name*” for “with assumptions”, and a hollow, dashed “≥ name (incomplete)” for a lower bound.

Zooming, highlighting and hovering

  • Scroll over the chart to zoom, and drag to pan. The slider under the chart does the same. Reset view goes back to the full range.
  • Hover anywhere for a tooltip with the date, the amount of every compound at that moment, any assumptions or missing data, whether CYP2D6 is inhibited, which QT-relevant compounds are present and any lingering effects.
  • Click a product in the legend, or one of its lines, to highlight it and fade the others. Click it again, or an empty part of the chart, to show all.

Display options

In Settings → Display:

  • Y-axis: Linear or Log. A log scale shows the long low tails, where most of the clearance time is spent.
  • Units: mg amount, or % of dose (percent of the most recent dose). Patches always stay in mg.
  • Show metabolites and Show range bands turn those off for a simpler view.
  • Show inactive metabolites is off by default. Metabolites classed as clinically inactive are then hidden; when shown they're faint, labelled “(inactive)”, and never hold up a clear date.

Where the library knows a metabolite's timing but not how much is formed, its curve is drawn dotted as a percentage of its own peak on a separate right-hand axis, labelled “% of own peak (timing only)”.

The legend

Clear statuses

A product is clear when the drug and every relevant metabolite it forms have fallen below the clear threshold (by default 5.5 half-lives, about 2% of each compound's peak). The status appears in the dose list, the legend (in short form), the tooltip and the PDF.

Clear by <date> — with assumptionsThe date relies on something the tool had to assume: an assumed spread around a single published value, the midpoint of a published range, an assumed phenotype effect, a steady-state switch, a conservative choice for partial data, or a metabolite treated as inactive before review.Hover over the status, or read the PDF's “Assumptions and missing data”, and decide whether each one is acceptable for this patient.

“Not clear within the simulated window” means the slowest part of the range is still above the threshold at the end of the simulation. An “Incomplete” product near the ibogaine date gets an amber warning, never a green light.

CYP2D6 track

Ibogaine is metabolised mainly by CYP2D6. Drugs that inhibit CYP2D6 can raise ibogaine exposure to resemble a poor metaboliser's. The CYP2D6 inhibition track shows when that is likely.

  • A bar is drawn while an inhibiting compound is above its clear threshold, coloured by strength: yellow for weak, orange for moderate, red for strong.
  • Reversible inhibition ends when the inhibitor clears. Mechanism-based inhibition destroys the enzyme, so a fading recovery tail follows while it is resynthesised. The tail lasts the clear threshold × the CYP2D6 recovery half-life (by default 5.5 × 51 h, about 12 days). The recovery half-life is set in Settings.
  • If inhibition overlaps the flood dose or a booster, a red warning says “CYP2D6 likely inhibited at treatment” and names the drugs. The ibogaine projection then uses poor-metaboliser values.
  • If a compound still present at the ibogaine date hasn't been assessed for CYP2D6 inhibition, an amber warning lists it. Unassessed is not the same as “no inhibition”.
  • When no inhibitor is present, the track reads “— none”.

QT lanes

  • Each compound with QT liability, or whose QT liability hasn't been assessed, gets its own lane, labelled on the left. Metabolite lanes are hidden when metabolites are hidden.
  • Colour shows the risk tier from CredibleMeds: red for known risk, orange for possible risk, yellow for conditional risk. A grey hatched lane means unassessed: no tier has been entered yet.
  • A lane is drawn while the compound is above its clear threshold. Its opacity follows the amount as a fraction of its own peak, so it fades as the drug clears.
  • The patient's baseline QTc is shown at the right of the track title.
  • Summed relative QT load (in Settings) adds the compounds together, weighted by their risk and amount. It is qualitative, never a predicted change in ms. It is only available when every compound in the lanes has been calibrated in the library, so it is often greyed out; hover over it to see which compounds are missing.

Lingering effects and receptor overlap

Some drugs have effects that outlast the drug itself, such as receptor down-regulation or depleted transmitters. Where the library records how long these last, the Lingering effects after clearance track shows a fading band from the compound's clear date. Hover for the library's note.

Below the chart, Receptor overlap at the ibogaine date lists receptors and transporters hit by compounds from two or more different products still present at the treatment date, with each compound's action.

Both are qualitative reminders taken from the literature, not modelled effects.

Custom drugs

If a drug isn't in the library, or has no data yet, you can enter it yourself from a published source. Use + Add custom drug under the search box, or the offer that appears when a search finds nothing. The wizard has eight steps:

  1. Basics: name, aliases, route, category, class, typical dose and molecular weight.
  2. Kinetics & release: the half-life (typical, min and max, or mark each unknown), an optional early phase and steady-state half-life, and how the product is taken (immediate, modified release, patch or depot), dose unit, salt factor and bioavailability.
  3. Metabolites: add metabolites, and metabolites of metabolites, with the fraction formed and their own kinetics, or link one already in the library.
  4. CYP2D6: whether it is a substrate or inhibitor, and how strongly.
  5. QT: the risk tier.
  6. Receptors: targets and actions.
  7. Lingering & nonlinear: effects that outlast clearance, and whether the kinetics are nonlinear.
  8. Source: the citation and a link.

Custom drugs belong to this patient's session: they are saved in this browser and in patient files, but not added to the shared library. They carry the user-entered badge everywhere, including the PDF. Settings → Custom drugs lists them; Copy as library entry produces the text needed to request that the drug be added.

Settings

Clearance

  • Clear threshold (half-lives): default 5.5, about 2% of each compound's peak remaining. The percentage is shown under the box.
  • CYP2D6 recovery half-life (h): default 51 h. How fast CYP2D6 is resynthesised after mechanism-based inhibition. Marked “verify”: check it against current literature.
  • Assumed spread when only one value is known (CV %): default 30%. Used where the library has a single half-life or a missing bound. Labelled “assumed spread” wherever it is used.
  • Treat unknown metabolites as formation-limited: off by default. Off, a product with a metabolite of unknown kinetics is “Incomplete”. On, that metabolite is assumed to clear as fast as it is formed, and the product is “with assumptions” instead.

Display

  • Y-axis and Units: see Reading the chart.
  • Show metabolites, Show inactive metabolites, Show range bands.
  • CYP2D6 inhibition track, QT liability track, Summed relative QT load overlay and Lingering effects track: show or hide each track.
  • Date format: day first or month first. Time: 24-hour or 12-hour.

Exports and data

  • Clinic name: printed at the top of PDF and PNG exports.
  • Custom drugs: list, copy or delete them.
  • Clear all data: see Privacy.

Saving, loading, exporting

  • Save file downloads the whole session (patient, doses, ibogaine plan, custom drugs and settings) as name-date.cleardate.json.
  • Load file opens a saved file and replaces what's on screen. If a custom drug in the file has the same ID as a library drug, it is renamed and you're told.
  • PDF creates a landscape A4 report in light colours. Page 1 has the clinic name, patient details, baseline QTc, CYP2D6 phenotype and the chart as currently zoomed. The following pages list the doses, the planned ibogaine, the clear dates (typical and slowest in the range), the Assumptions and missing data for each product, and the warnings. The disclaimer is printed at the foot of every page.
  • PNG saves an image with the same header, the chart and up to eight warnings.

Assumptions and missing data lists, per product: incomplete clear dates and the missing compounds; every assumption (assumed spreads and range midpoints, assumed phenotype effects, steady-state switches, conservative choices for partial data); metabolites excluded as inactive; branches not formed for the patient's phenotype; formation-limited conjugates, which can accumulate in renal impairment; amounts given on an administered basis; and whether QT, CYP2D6 or receptor data is unassessed.

How the model works

Amount remaining and half-lives

For each dose, ClearDate estimates how much of the drug is left in the body over time. Most drugs are eliminated at a rate proportional to the amount present (first-order elimination), so the amount halves every half-life. After 5.5 half-lives about 2% is left, about 98% eliminated: that is the default clear threshold. You can change it in Settings.

With repeated doses, the amounts add up. The clear threshold is measured against each compound's own peak in this patient's regimen.

Metabolites

Many drugs are converted into metabolites, some of which are active and some of which clear more slowly than the drug itself. ClearDate follows the chain: the fraction of each compound that becomes each metabolite, and each metabolite's own half-life. A metabolite shared by two drugs in the same chain, like oxazepam below, is tracked once per product. A product isn't clear until the drug and every relevant metabolite are below the threshold.

Some conjugates (for example glucuronides) clear faster than they are formed. These are formation-limited: they are plotted tracking their parent, and labelled as such, because they can accumulate when the kidneys are impaired.

Two phases, steady state, patches and depots

  • Two-phase drugs clear part of each dose quickly and the rest slowly. Both phases are modelled when the library has them.
  • Steady state: some drugs clear more slowly (or quickly) after weeks of regular dosing. Once a regimen has run long enough, the steady-state half-life is used, and the tooltip and PDF say so.
  • Patches, infusions and depots release the drug into the body gradually. ClearDate models the release as its own step before elimination, so the drug keeps entering the body for a while after a patch is removed or long after a depot injection.

CYP2D6 phenotype

For drugs cleared by CYP2D6, the library records how the half-life changes in poor, intermediate and ultrarapid metabolisers, and how much of each metabolite is formed. ClearDate applies the patient's phenotype. When the phenotype is unknown, the range band runs from the ultrarapid to the poor metaboliser. Where a metabolite is not formed at all in a phenotype, its branch is left out and the chart notes “not formed in … metabolisers”.

Clinical relevance of metabolites

Every metabolite in the library is classed as active, inactive or unknown. Drugs taken and active metabolites always count towards the clear date. Unknown ones count too, so an unclassified metabolite with unpublished kinetics makes the product “Incomplete” rather than silently ignored. Inactive metabolites never hold up the clear date; when that classification hasn't been reviewed yet, the status becomes “with assumptions”.

The conservative rule

When data is only partly published, ClearDate uses whichever option gives the later clear date, and labels it. For example, a fast early phase without a known size is ignored, so the whole dose follows the slower half-life. When it isn't known how long a drug takes to reach steady state, a repeated regimen is simulated both ways and the later date counts.

Range bands and assumed spread

Where the literature gives a range of half-lives, the band covers it. Where only a range is published, the typical value is taken as the geometric midpoint. Where only one value is published, ClearDate assumes a spread around it (30% by default) and labels it “assumed spread”.

Limitations

  • It estimates the amount in the body, not plasma concentrations.
  • Ordinary oral doses are treated as absorbed immediately; absorption delays are not modelled.
  • There are no adjustments for liver or kidney impairment, age, pregnancy or body composition.
  • Drug–drug interactions are not modelled, apart from the CYP2D6 effect on the ibogaine projection.
  • Nonlinear kinetics (for example at high doses) are flagged with ⚠ and a warning, but not modelled.
  • Lingering effects and receptor overlap are qualitative reminders, not predictions.

The drug library and its sources

Drug values are entered by hand from published sources, with the citations recorded in the library. In a verified entry, every section has been reviewed against those sources. Entries still being reviewed are marked unverified or draft wherever they appear, including the PDF.

Primary sources:

  • FDA prescribing information via DailyMed, and EU and UK product information (EMA, electronic medicines compendium).
  • Baselt, Disposition of Toxic Drugs and Chemicals in Man.
  • The Flockhart CYP table and the FDA drug-interaction tables.
  • CredibleMeds for QT risk tiers, entered by hand with a citation and used under their terms.
  • CPIC and ClinPGx for pharmacogenomics.
  • The IUPHAR/BPS Guide to Pharmacology and the PDSP Ki Database for receptor targets.
  • The primary pharmacokinetic literature via PubMed.

Review and verification

Each entry is reviewed section by section (half-life, metabolites, CYP2D6, QT, receptors and so on). A section can be confirmed, confirmed as unknown when the data genuinely isn't published, or marked not applicable. An entry is verified only when every section has been reviewed. Where the data is genuinely unpublished, as it is for many metabolites, the tool says so instead of guessing: the entry shows verified — incomplete data and its clear dates are lower bounds.

What's in the library

The library currently holds drugs as products (each route and formulation is a product), made up of compounds including metabolites. products and compounds are verified so far. Last updated .

Categories covered: opioids and opioid use disorder treatments; benzodiazepines and sedatives; antidepressants; antipsychotics; mood stabilisers and anticonvulsants; stimulants; psychedelics, entactogens and dissociatives; cardiovascular drugs; antimicrobials, antiemetics and other interactors.

The library will keep growing. Requests for new drugs, corrections and source suggestions are welcome: see Requests and feedback.

FAQ

Why does a drug show “Incomplete”?
The drug, or a metabolite it forms that may be active, has no published kinetics in the library. The date shown is the earliest the product could be clear. Hover over the status to see which compound is missing.
Why is the clear date later than I expected?
Usually because of a long-lived metabolite (for example nordiazepam from diazepam, or norfluoxetine from fluoxetine), a slower half-life at steady state, slow release from a patch or depot, or the conservative rule for partial data. Hover over the curve to see which compound is still present, and check the assumptions.
Why isn't a drug listed?
It hasn't been added yet, or it is listed under another name: try the brand or generic name. You can enter it as a custom drug and request it for the library.
Why can't I select a drug that is listed?
It shows “no data yet”: it is in the library but has no half-life yet.
Does it account for kidney or liver disease?
No. There are no adjustments for organ function, age or pregnancy. Formation-limited conjugates, which can accumulate in renal impairment, are flagged.
Why amounts instead of blood levels?
The amount remaining needs fewer assumptions, and is what matters for whether a drug has cleared. Blood levels also depend on distribution and protein binding, which vary widely between patients and aren't consistently published.
Is any data sent anywhere?
No. Everything stays in your browser. The app only downloads its own files and the drug library.
Can I use it offline once it's loaded?
Yes, as long as the page stays open: nothing is fetched after loading. Reloading the page needs a connection, unless your browser has cached it.
What does “verified — incomplete data” mean?
The entry has been fully reviewed, and the missing kinetics are confirmed as unpublished. Its clear dates are lower bounds, and a library update won't change that until new research is published.
Will closing the browser lose my patient?
No: the session is autosaved in this browser and is there next time. It is lost only if you use Clear all data or clear the browser's site data. Save a file for a permanent copy.
How do I correct an entry?
Email the drug, the value you think is wrong and a source. See Requests and feedback.

Requests and feedback

To request a drug, report an error or suggest a source, email ian@ethovoa.com.

Please include:

  • the drug name, and brand or street names if relevant;
  • the route and formulation (for example oral extended release, patch, depot injection);
  • ideally a source: a prescribing information document, review article or study;
  • for a custom drug you've already entered, the text from Settings → Custom drugs → Copy as library entry.

Never include patient details in a request.
